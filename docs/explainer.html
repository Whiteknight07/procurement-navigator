How Procurement Navigator works, from the ground up

Read top to bottom. Each chapter adds one idea, and every example is real output from the Acme and Northstar deals as of 3 November 2026.

Chapter 1

The situation

Lantern AI sells an AI assistant to large companies. Before a bank or a telecom can buy it, their risk teams run a vendor review. That review is what stalls the deal.

Two deals, one person

Acme National Bank sent a 60-row questionnaire on 14 September and emails from its privacy and risk staff. Its October pilot target has already passed.

Northstar Telecom has its own questionnaire and a launch target of 16 November, 13 days away.

Jai, Lantern's commercial lead, runs both and approves every answer that goes out.

What Jai told us hurts

  • Questions come in rounds (20, then 15) with repeats, additions and reused question numbers.
  • The answers are scattered across Slack, email, texts, drive files and past deals.
  • Facts change mid-deal, and the customer has to be told straight away.
  • Nobody keeps a central record of what each customer was told.
  • Evidence goes stale: a pen test from six months ago will not do.

What the system must show, for each deal

What is requiredEvery distinct need, from every round and email
What we knowBacked by evidence, with the quote
What is unresolvedUnknown, unconfirmed, conflicting, undecided
What is blockingThe root cause, not just the symptom
What to do nextWho acts, and what it unlocks

These five become the five main tabs of the Deal screen. The rule above all of them: never invent an answer, and show conflicts instead of hiding them.

Chapter 3

Five words to know

Everything in the system is one of these. Read them left to right: what arrives, what it says, what is true, what the customer needs, what someone does. Pick one.

AskOne place a customer asked for something: a questionnaire row, a sentence in an email, or a topic the guidance predicts. 184 in total.
CatalogThe fixed list of 32 facts the system knows how to track, each with an owner, a proof type, an age limit and dependencies.
LedgerThe record of what Jai sent and who he asked. Each sent answer keeps a snapshot of the facts it relied on.
Chapter 4

Follow one answer: Acme's log retention

One thread, from the customer's question to an answer Jai can send. Use Next, or pick a step.

The customer asks for the same thing in two places

Acme's questionnaire row Q-004 says State retention for source files, prompts, responses, logs and traces. Separately, Elena from Acme Privacy writes in an email asking to clarify retention by data type. Both mean: tell us how long you keep each kind of data.

If we kept them apart
Jai gets two to-dos for one need, may answer it twice with two different numbers, and cannot tell when the need is really covered. Elena's "by data type" gets lost.
So we merge them
One requirement, acme:Q-004 Retention by data type, answered once. Elena's wording is kept as how Acme reads the question: Acme wants retention broken down by data type.

Who decides: the model, in one call per deal. It sees every ask from that customer (questionnaire rows, email sentences, predicted topics) and groups the ones that are the same need. Code then checks that every ask landed in exactly one requirement and that the model used only ids and facts it was shown. Jai can see which asks went into each requirement on the Deal screen.

Then what: the requirement lists the facts an answer needs. For Q-004 there are four: retention of source files, prompts and responses, app logs, and traces. Each fact gets its own status (next steps). When all four are verified, the requirement is ready and Jai sends one answer that covers both places. We follow one fact: app logs.

The questionnaire row was read by code, not the model. The email ask was pulled out by the model, and code checked that the quote really is in the email.

What we have on file about app-log retention

A statement is what one source says: one quote, from one place. The question is the thing we want to know, Acme's app-log retention. Here, four statements about one question, with two different values. Keeping them apart is what lets the system show disagreement instead of picking one.

None of these settles it. Retention is settled by the owner (Engineering) saying so, and nobody here is the owner talking about Acme. The kind of source matters: a plan is what was intended, not proof it is live; a hint (a generic doc, another customer, a typed-in draft) is shown but never the answer. More in chapter 6.

Chapter 6

How a question becomes a fact

Two things only. A statement is what one source says. A question is something we need to know, like Acme's app-log retention. When the right person or document settles a question, it becomes a fact. A fact is always verified; if it isn't verified, it isn't a fact yet, and the system says why.

Whose word counts

From strongest to weakest. A stronger statement beats a weaker one; between equals, the newest wins.

DocA documentA document in the evidence store (Vanta). For questions only the customer can answer, the customer's own words. Example: SOC 2 Type II, Jan-Dec 2025: FINAL.
OwnerThe ownerThe person who owns that area says it as settled, on Slack, in notes or by email. Example: Anthony, App logs are 14 days.
PlanA plan or chatDeal documents and drafts, or chat from someone who is not the owner. Useful, but it can't settle anything on its own. Example: App logs intended 14 days.
HintA hintA generic product doc, a draft answer typed into the questionnaire, or another customer's answer. Shown to Jai, never the answer. Example: Standard application logs: 30 days.

What settles each question

Jai set this once per question in the catalog. It is the rule that stops a Slack message from settling SOC 2.

A documentSOC 2, pen test, insurance. Only the document itself settles them. A Slack message saying the new report is final cannot.
The ownerStorage region, log retention, model providers. The owner saying so settles them (a document also does). This is how Jai checks them today.
The customerAcme's residency rule, the pilot's data scope. Only the customer's own words settle them; our guess is just a plan.

Six checks, in order

The first one that matches gives the question its status. Only the last one makes it a fact.

Has anyone whose word counts said anything? (Hints don't count.)If not: UnknownAcme residency requirement: Nothing usable says anything about this. Only Acme can answer.
Does someone say it is not decided yet?Then: UndecidedAcme fallback routing. Anthony: for Acme probably disable if residency/legal requires it. Not finalized.
Do two statements that count disagree?Then: ConflictingPen test remediation. The owner says Remediated; evidence needed; the evidence on file says no remediation evidence. Get updated evidence.
Did the right person or document say it?If not: UnconfirmedAcme app-log retention: there is a plan, but the owner has not confirmed it.
Has it expired, or is it older than this question allows?Then: Out of datePen test: dated Feb 2026, 275 days old, policy allows 183. Insurance: expired on 1 October 2026.
OtherwiseFactSOC 2: settled by the Vanta report, with the note Also on file: 2026 audit not yet final.

A newer but weaker statement that disagrees does not win; it makes the question conflicting. An older statement replaced by a newer one from the same kind of source is just out of date, not a conflict.

Requirements get a status too

ReadyEvery fact it needs is verified. Jai can draft and approve. OpenSome facts are not verified yet; work can start on them. BlockedA fact it needs is waiting on another fact (chapter 7). Missing textPlaceholder rows with nothing to answer, such as 37 rows reading Control 24: provide the customer-specific answer. PredictedA topic the guidance says usually comes up, not asked yet. Becomes open when the customer asks. Umbrella, unmappedA whole-form ask covered by its parts; or a need that no catalog fact covers, shown to Jai rather than guessed.

Chapter 9

Writing and approving an answer

Jai picks a ready requirement. The model drafts; code checks; Jai reads and approves. Here is the real draft for Acme's Q-007, Provide current SOC 2 or equivalent assurance.

Lantern AI Technologies has a final SOC 2 Type II report for January through December 2025.
Cites the evidence store: SOC 2 Type II, Jan-Dec 2025: FINAL.
The 2026 audit is not yet final.
Cites the evidence store: 2026 audit is not yet final.

Code checks before Approve is enabled

  • Every answer sentence cites at least one statement.
  • Every cited statement settles a fact this requirement needs.
  • Nothing cites a hint: another customer, a generic doc or a questionnaire draft.
  • Open questions appear only as pending lines naming the owner, and a pending line may not leak the best guess.
  • If the facts changed since the draft was made, Approve is disabled until it is redrafted.

Result for this draft: no errors, so Jai can approve. When he does, the ledger records the text and a snapshot of the SOC 2 fact as it was.

Chapter 11

The four screens

A Streamlit app over the same state. Run it with .venv/bin/streamlit run app.py.

Monday briefing

What changed since the last rebuild, each deal's state, Jai's to-dos, root blockers, one message per person with what it unlocks, waits, delays and updates owed.

Deal

Seven tabs: what is required, what we know, what is unresolved, what is blocking, what to do next, questions (every statement about one, with its source and quote), and rounds (how round 2 matched round 1).

Approvals

Pick a ready requirement, draft it, read each sentence with its cited quote, and approve and send. Approve is disabled while any check fails.

Add input

Add one of the six demo inputs, or paste your own email, Slack message or questionnaire. The state rebuilds and the briefing shows what changed. Move the as-of date to watch deadlines and expiry shift.

Chapter 12

Keeping it honest

How we know it does what it says, and where it does not yet.

36 of 36 eval checks

An answer key written from reading the case: statuses, conflicts, round matches, flags, and every step of the demo story.

47 unit tests

Rules, stable ids, round matching, impact ranking and red-team cases, all with no network.

Red team

Prompt injection, fake evidence, broken files, conflicting sources, future dates. Five real bugs found and fixed.

A third customer

Harbor Regional Health, a hospital with HIPAA needs. It ran with no code change; healthcare facts needed catalog entries only.

Cached model calls

Same input, same output, offline. Evals prove the rules, not yet how much the model varies between runs.

Known limits

Withdrawn requirements stay open; a bridge letter looks ready; adding a catalog fact re-reads everything.
