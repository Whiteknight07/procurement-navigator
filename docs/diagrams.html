Architecture and data flow

Two views of the same system. The architecture shows the parts and who decides at each one. The data flow diagram (DFD) shows what data moves between them and where it is kept.

What "Jai's list" is

The catalog, config/catalog.yaml. It lists the things procurement questions boil down to, with Jai's rules for each. It has 33 entries: 14 are about the company (SOC 2, insurance), so there is one answer for everyone; 19 are per deal (storage region, log retention), so there is one answer per customer. 14 + 19 × 2 deals = 52 questions. We wrote it from the case material (Jai's notes, the questionnaires, the ownership sheet). In real use, Jai owns it and edits it.

2. Data flow diagram

A level-1 DFD in the usual notation: external entities are people and systems outside ours (square boxes), processes transform data (numbered, rounded), data stores are where data rests (open-ended boxes, D1 to D4), and every arrow is labelled with the data that moves. Asks, statements, requirements and questions are not stores: they are rebuilt in memory every time.
